# Odia resource research — 12 October 2026

Research scope: seven supplied URLs and narrowly selected alternatives for Roman→Odia typing, word ranking, English→Odia lookup, and contemporary loanwords. No corpus/model bulk downloads or third-party data imports were performed. Pinned source links and verification details appear below. Statements below distinguish directly read source documentation from unverified assumptions; reported benchmark scores are upstream claims, not independently rerun results.

## What matters for this app

Three different products need different data:

1. **Roman Odia typing** needs variant Roman→Odia pairs (`pani` → `ପାଣି`), prefix recall, inflection coverage, and good ranking.
2. **English meaning lookup** needs headword, sense, part of speech, translation, example, and provenance (`water` → `ପାଣି`/`ଜଳ`). A monolingual corpus cannot directly supply this structure. A translation model can propose entries, but generated entries require review.
3. **Loanword typing** needs approved borrowed spellings (`phone` → `ଫୋନ୍`) and variants, distinct from the semantic translation (`ଦୂରଭାଷ`). These illustrative forms are editorial proposals, not extracted dataset evidence. Native review should settle variants, terminal halants, and colloquial/formal labels.

Do not silently substitute an English dictionary meaning into ordinary Roman Odia typing: e.g. Roman Odia `mate` can mean `ମତେ`, while English “mate” has unrelated senses. Offer clearly labelled meaning/loanword results through an explicit English lookup mode; keep transliteration and literal-English options available.

## Stronger near-term alternatives, verified through public GitHub documentation

### 1. AI4Bharat IndicXlit / Aksharantar — highest direct relevance to Roman typing

Source: [IndicXlit README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicXlit), directly retrieved `master/README.md`.

The README documents a roughly 11M-parameter transliteration model, 21 supported languages including Oriya (`ori`), and Aksharantar's claimed 26 million Roman/native word pairs across 20 languages. It states **MIT for code and models**; that statement should not be silently extended to the dataset. The [Aksharantar public card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/Aksharantar/blob/e418c1fc928d9f5393af33268472cf20c1891be8/README.md) is now verified at SHA `e418c1fc928d9f5393af33268472cf20c1891be8`. It declares Odia (`ori-en`) **346K train / 3K validation / 4,256 test** entries, not independently counted. Fields are `unique_identifier`, `native word`, `english word` (explicitly **Romanization**, not English meaning), `source`, and `score` (model character-level log probability). The card's example is Hindi, not an Odia quality sample. Licensing distinguishes manually collected data **CC-BY** (visible prose does not specify version) and mined/existing sources **CC0**, with an explicit caveat that authors do not own the extracted text and CC0 covers mined-data packaging. The generic card metadata `license: cc` is insufficient by itself; preserve per-source provenance and verify applicable attribution/upstream terms. The README reports Odia Roman→native top-1 **58.90% native words**, **30.63% named entities** on its Aksharantar benchmarks; those upstream figures warn against treating model adoption as solved quality. They are not measurements of this app or directly comparable to an internal app corpus.

Action: acquire a pinned and licensed Odia subset, audit short/common/colloquial forms and names, preserve genuine Roman spelling variants, and evaluate candidate recall/top-1 against a native-reviewed holdout. The compact model is worth comparing as a fallback candidate generator, while the explicit lexicon and user preferences retain control. Do not ship the whole multilingual corpus or model without measuring runtime size/latency.

### 2. Sense-aware English→Odia: IndoWordNet, Wiktionary, and a reviewed local seed

- [IndoWordNet catalog link](http://www.cfilt.iitb.ac.in/indowordnet/) and [pyiwn](https://github.com/anoopkunchukuttan/pyiwn): README documents synsets, glosses, examples and semantic relations. Its `LANGUAGES.md` includes English and Oriya. Its wrapper `LICENSE.txt` is MIT, **which does not establish the underlying WordNet data's redistribution rights or English↔Odia alignment coverage**. Verify those and preserve sense IDs before importing.
- [IndoWordnet Parallel Corpus](https://github.com/anoopkunchukuttan/indowordnet_parallel): README explicitly states approximately 6.3M parallel segments across 18 Indian languages, mined from glosses/examples, and **CC BY-SA 4.0**. It recommends v0.2 and calls v0.1 buggy. This is related sentence material, not proof of a ready English→Odia dictionary. Its license and alignment should not be generalized to the full original WordNet.
- [Wiktextract](https://github.com/tatuylonen/wiktextract): README directly verifies a machine-readable extraction path including word senses, POS, `translations` entries carrying language, word and sense labels, with code under MIT. English Wiktionary translations filtered to language code `or` are a plausible dictionary seed; initial access was blocked, but the follow-up below now provides actual counts and samples from Kaikki. Source content licenses/attribution and current dump terms remain separate from extractor MIT.
- Most reliable immediate shippable baseline: a small **original, native-reviewed, sense-labelled** set for everyday topics and loanwords. Mark it a seed, not a comprehensive dictionary. Expand with independently licensed data after measured review.

Recommended dictionary schema: `id`, `englishHeadword`, `sense`, `partOfSpeech`, `odiaForms[]`, `romanAliases[]`, `kind: meaning|loan`, `register: conversational|formal`, `example`, `source`, `sourceRevision`, `license`, `reviewStatus`. Do not merge all forms with identical English headwords into a single unlabelled candidate. Search should prioritize exact English headword/alias over prefixes and show sense labels; selecting a result should insert an explicitly chosen form.

### 3. AI4Bharat IndicTrans2 / BPCC — translation assistance, not automatic dictionary truth

Source: [IndicTrans2 README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicTrans2), directly retrieved `main/README.md`.

README includes Odia `ory_Orya`, English `eng_Latn`, 1B models and distilled English↔Indic 200M models. Model checkpoints are stated MIT. Newly added BPCC-H-Wiki/BPCC-H-Daily and IN22 sets are stated CC BY 4.0. Crucially, mined/existing corpora are packaged under CC0 with an explicit statement that authors **do not own the text**; CC0 packaging is not blanket clearance of upstream text. No dataset rows or Odia counts were sampled.

Use human-created daily/conversational material for candidate evaluation and reviewed bilingual examples; translation models may draft meanings for review. Avoid interpreting sentence-alignment or generated translations as perfect word-sense alignment. Keep test material out of imported training/ranking data. A 200M translation model is still inappropriate to add casually to a mobile offline bundle.

### 4. IndicNLP / IndicCorp frequency resources — useful ranking lead, with limitations

Source: [AI4Bharat IndicNLP Corpus README](https://github.com/ai4bharat-indicnlp/indicnlp_corpus), directly retrieved `master/README.md`.

README reports Odia: 0.69M documents, 6.94M sentences, 107M tokens, and describes word-frequency files as tab-delimited word/count. These are upstream published counts, not fresh measurements. License section states **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. Stronger fit for priors than OCR imagery or LLM instructions, but news/crawl counts can overrank formal language, names, boilerplate, and errors. Do not use frequency as a gold spelling list or import into a commercial product under an assumed permissive license.

## Recommended ingestion and evaluation plan

1. Pin source revision and store provenance/license alongside every import; keep handwritten overrides separate from generated artifacts.
2. Normalize NFC, detect invisible/control characters and invalid combining sequences without stripping meaningful marks; deduplicate canonical keys; preserve distinct meaningful word forms and Roman aliases.
3. Segment data by conversational/formal/news/literary/domain source. Inspect stratified samples before deriving frequencies. A crawl or OCR corpus should not dictate conversational top-1 results.
4. Distinguish Romanization aliases, morphological forms, semantic English translations and borrowed-word forms in data and UI. Suggest all appropriate forms; do not force one “pure” Odia replacement for a normal loanword.
5. Maintain a frozen native-reviewed sentence/word benchmark with different writers and common Roman variants; measure exact-form recall@5, top-1 accuracy, keystrokes/corrections per sentence, unwanted replacements, and candidate latency p50/p95. Include code-mixed messages, names, inflections, punctuation and backspacing. Test the actual UI commit path, not just engine output.
6. Rank with reviewed exact aliases + lexical priors + light context; keep correction/personal preference reversible. Only evaluate learned model fallback after baseline failure categories are fixed.

Priority order: stabilize commit/edit flow and Roman variant coverage → reviewed everyday/loanword seed with explicit English mode → licensed IndicXlit/Aksharantar comparison → modest context ranking from audited text → optional translation assistance/OCR features. Bigger pretraining corpora alone will not make the app seamless.

### Kaikki / English Wiktionary: a feasible candidate-review source

- [Odia dictionary index](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Odia/index.html) identifies the source as English Wiktionary dump **2026-09-02**, extracted **2026-10-03**, using Wiktextract `1a05e46f9efbccda6a2b2f8e21b30a9c0c46513a` and wikitextprocessor `e3d6d4edb77618f4d6680edc66e3f774bea59820`.
- Downloaded [Odia JSONL](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Odia/kaikki.org-dictionary-Odia.jsonl), **4,397,359 bytes**, and independently counted **2,211 entries, 2,034 distinct headword strings, 4,898 senses**. Entries can repeat a headword by part of speech; these are not 4,898 distinct vocabulary words. SHA-256: `bde773876ec15b22577adaf0d0901ed28c0eaf52394b0dd839f643b3cbc3a939`.
- This extract is **Odia headwords with English glosses**, including POS, sense IDs, scholarly romanizations, alternatives, examples and etymology. Observed examples: `ପାଣି` has separate water/liquid/drink senses; `ଘର` has house/home, shelter and family senses; `ଫୋନ` has alternative form `ଫୋନ୍` and the example `ମତେ ଫୋନ କର।`; `କମ୍ପ୍ୟୁଟର` has gloss “computer.” These are observed source contents, not native-speaker approval of every spelling or definition. Scholarly romanizations such as `pāṇi` are not automatically the aliases people type on phones.
- [English senses tagged “Terms with Odia translations”](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/categories-other/91/Terms%20with%20Odia%20translations/index.html) reports **2,590 word senses** and offers a **96.0 MB** postprocessed extract. This is a website category count, not independently counted bilingual pairs: disambiguation can be imperfect, entries can include translations to many languages, and tags alone do not establish an Odia translation field on every sense.
- Three individual English JSONL entries were sampled directly: [water](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/meaning/w/wa/water.jsonl) has Odia `ଜଳ` and `ପାଣି` under sense “inorganic compound H₂O”; [bank](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/meaning/b/ba/bank.jsonl) has `ବାଙ୍କ୍` under sense “institution”; [phone](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/meaning/p/ph/phone.jsonl) has **no Odia translation field** in the fetched three entries, although the Odia-side extract contains `ଫୋନ`. This proves coverage is asymmetric. The Odia-side bank entry is `ବ୍ୟାଙ୍କ୍`, so even this tiny sample exposes a spelling choice needing native review.
- Direct English translation fields include `word`, `sense`, `code`/`lang_code: or`, and `roman`. That is the preferable starting path for an English→Odia import. Never automatically reverse all English gloss prose: a gloss is a definition, not always a usable English headword or an exact translation relation.
- Kaikki explicitly marks the postprocessed JSONL format **deprecated**, points consumers to raw Wiktextract data, and says some additional data from other sources is merged in postprocessing. Its complete raw English-edition extract is shown as **23.9 GB / 2.8 GB compressed**, so blindly downloading it in a browser/app is inappropriate. Use a pinned offline extraction job or small individually reviewed entries, and record the source page and sense for each result.
- **License boundary:** Wiktextract's MIT license covers the extractor. The fetched Kaikki pages do not provide a complete data-license grant; direct Wikimedia terms/copyright pages still returned HTTP 403 from their hosts even with network access available. English Wiktionary content normally carries Wikimedia attribution/share-alike terms, but the exact applicable dump terms and any merged-source obligations were not directly verified here. Resolve them and retain attribution/share-alike compliance before shipping imported data; do not label this dataset MIT or assume proprietary redistribution is unrestricted.

**Recommendation:** Build a small offline review queue from explicitly labelled English entries' `or` translations, preserving English sense/POS, Odia form, source URL, dump revision, and license. Supplement missing cases from Odia glosses only through editorial review. Show spelling conflicts and contemporary loanword alternatives to the owner; approve individual entries into the app. This is a tractable next step toward a few thousand reviewed senses, not evidence of a comprehensive dictionary. Keep the app's existing original seed and provenance separate.

### IndoWordNet: verified sense structure, incomplete alignment and unresolved reuse terms

The [live IndoWordNet site](https://www.cfilt.iitb.ac.in/indowordnet/) is accessible. Its English `water` search reports **8 synsets**, while Odia `ପାଣି` reports **4**; these are search-result counts, not language-wide vocabulary counts.

A [live Odia search](https://www.cfilt.iitb.ac.in/indowordnet/wordnet?langno=18&query=%E0%AC%AA%E0%AC%BE%E0%AC%A3%E0%AC%BF) provides synset **1930**, POS **NOUN**, synonyms `ଜଳ, ପାଣି, ନୀର, ଅମ୍ବୁ, ସଲିଳ`, an Odia gloss, example `ଜଳ ହିଁ ଜୀବନର ଆଧାର`, and an English water-compound gloss. Another sense in the same response explicitly says **“English Linkage Not Available”**. Therefore, sense-aware bilingual enrichment is possible for some entries, but universal English alignment cannot be assumed. Literary synonyms also need register labels and conversational ranking.

The pyiwn implementation points to an approximately **80 MB** external WordNet archive (not downloaded) and parses synset IDs, lemma lists, POS, glosses and examples; it is not an already reviewed English→Odia app dictionary. The [CFILT Hindi WordNet download page](https://www.cfilt.iitb.ac.in/wordnet/webhwn/downloaderInfo.php) explicitly states **“For commercial use, Please contact Dr. Pushpak Bhattacharyya”**. This page concerns Hindi WordNet and does not establish the exact Odia data terms, but it is positive evidence against assuming the wrapper's MIT license clears all underlying language data. The previously cited CC BY-SA license for the separate mined parallel corpus must also not be generalized to the original WordNet.

**Recommendation:** Treat IndoWordNet as a source to evaluate for reviewed synsets, definitions and examples, after obtaining its applicable Odia/English data-reuse terms and permitted export path. Keep cross-language synset linkage coverage as an explicit metric. It is a more meaningful dictionary source than pretraining text, but is not yet cleared for direct app bundling.
